# Tastee Cafe — Research Review 1
Research date: 2026-11-17. Status: **working research; design decisions are proposed, not customer-validated**.

## Research question
How should a hypothetical student-oriented café website help visitors discover drinks and decide whether the space fits their needs?

## Research → proposed design → planned verification

| Evidence / assumption | Proposed feature or decision | Check (not yet conducted) |
|---|---|---|
| Dunkin' groups its menu into visible categories (observed). | Keep any future Tastee Cafe menu easy to scan by category; only show sample items clearly labeled as examples. | Ask a reviewer to find a drink and a food item, then note any confusing labels. |
| Gregorys links visitors directly to its menu and ordering path (observed). | Give the proposed site a visible menu path; keep ordering language out unless ordering actually exists. | Ask a reviewer to find the menu and identify any wording that implies a real service. |

| Persona A prioritizes practical information (assumption). | Make menu navigation easy to find; mark unknown hours and location as placeholders. | Ask a reviewer to find the menu and identify missing operational facts. |
| Persona B wants to judge atmosphere (assumption). | Use a café story and visual mood section, with images labeled conceptual. | Ask what the reviewer infers about the café and whether any claim reads as fact. |
| No real menu prices or location are verified. | Do not present invented prices, address, hours, inventory, or services as real. | Scan for unsupported real-world claims. |

## Two positioning alternatives
1. **Student-first, accessible café**: straightforward menu, welcoming study-friendly atmosphere, and clear practical information. Assumption: students value this more than premium presentation.
2. **Premium specialty café**: craft-focused storytelling, curated drinks, polished imagery. Assumption: customers will pay more for an elevated experience.

**Provisional direction:** student-first, because it aligns with the intended student audience and supports a simpler information architecture. This is a design hypothesis, not a research-proven winner.

## Two persona hypotheses (not interviews)
- **Persona A — between-classes student:** wants a quick look at drinks, indicative prices, hours, and whether studying is welcome. No real customer interviewed.
- **Persona B — social visitor:** wants photos, atmosphere, and options for meeting friends. No real customer interviewed.

## Brand and persuasion
Character: warm, approachable, calm, student-friendly. Proposed palette: espresso brown, cream, and muted green. Honest persuasion: show clear options and benefits without fake reviews, fake scarcity, unverified discounts, or claims of real customer interviews.
